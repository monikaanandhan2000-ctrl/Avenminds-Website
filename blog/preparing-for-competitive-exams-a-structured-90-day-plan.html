Preparing for Competitive Exams: A Structured 90-Day Plan

Exam prep without a structure tends to drift toward whatever feels comfortable. Here's a 90-day framework that forces coverage and repetition.

EdTech & Training AvenMinds Team

The most common failure mode in exam preparation isn't lack of effort, it's lack of structure — hours spent on the subject that already feels comfortable, while weaker areas get avoided because they're harder to face. A 90-day plan works because it forces coverage of everything, on a schedule, whether it feels good or not.

We break the window into three phases: the first 30 days for full syllabus coverage at a fast pace, prioritizing breadth over depth; the next 30 for targeted practice on identified weak areas, using timed mock tests to actually find them rather than guessing; the final 30 for full-length simulated exams under real time pressure, with error review after each one.

The single highest-leverage habit in this plan is the error log — writing down not just what was answered wrong, but why, every single time. Most candidates repeat the same category of mistake for weeks without realizing it, and a structured review is what actually breaks the pattern before exam day.
